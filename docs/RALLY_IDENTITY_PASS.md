# Rally Identity Pass

This document is the production plan for turning the conversion into a rally game rather than a vehicle swap.

## Diagnosis

The rejected model used a constant-width side-profile extrusion. Its body was approximately 3.24 m long around a 2.56 m-class wheelbase, with an upright 1.1 m cabin above a chassis whose contact patch is about 0.66 m below the origin. That produced three linked failures:

1. the length-to-wheelbase ratio was toy-like;
2. the greenhouse was too tall and rectangular;
3. the body did not taper in plan view, so every camera angle read as a slab.

The course, camera, collision and dust systems also still carry assumptions from a narrow two-wheel downhill game.

## Reference envelope

The shipping car is fictional, but its envelope follows modern gravel rally proportions:

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---:|
| Overall length | 4.10 m |
| Width at body | 1.86 m |
| Wheelbase | 2.56 m |
| Gravel tyre diameter | 0.70 m |
| Roof height from contact patch | 1.46 m |
| Length / wheelbase | 1.60 |

The values are intentionally between current Rally2 and Rally1 examples rather than copying a branded car.

## Tool decision

### Shipping geometry

All production geometry remains generated by TypeScript. No GLB, FBX, OBJ, downloaded model, texture or photogrammetry asset ships with the game.

The main body is a longitudinal loft. Each station controls sill, shoulder, belt, roof and crown widths independently. This allows all three silhouette axes to be authored:

- side: rake, roof arc, hood and hatch;
- front/rear: shoulder width, cabin tuck and stance;
- plan: tapered nose/tail and wheel-arch emphasis.

Separate generated geometry supplies conformed glass, arch ribbons, lamps, aero, mudflaps and gravel wheels.

### Blender

Blender is optional development tooling only. A deterministic Python/BMesh script may be added later to preview the same station data, apply temporary bevel/weighted-normal studies and render orthographic sheets. The game must still run with only `npm install && npm run dev`.

A general-purpose Blender MCP is not a project dependency. It executes powerful Python in the host application and adds installation, security and reproducibility risk. If used by a developer, it is a private local review tool, never part of CI or runtime.

### Image-to-3D systems

Meshy, Tripo, TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D, TripoSR and InstantMesh can be useful for disposable shape studies. Their output is not suitable as the final asset here because:

- generated topology is not deterministic enough for the ink/prepass pipeline;
- most outputs assume PBR materials and texture baking;
- single-view reconstruction invents hidden surfaces;
- service/API output would violate the zero-external-assets rule if shipped;
- local high-quality models require substantial GPU and Python infrastructure.

They may only be used to compare broad silhouettes, never copied into the repository.

## Implementation phases

### 1. Proportion-led body — in progress

- replace constant-width extrusion with a full 3D station loft;
- use a 4.10 m envelope and 2.56 m visual wheelbase;
- lower the greenhouse and rake both windshield and hatch;
- put maximum width over the axles, not through the doors;
- expose the wheels beyond the body shoulder;
- add generated arch ribbons and a compact roof-edge wing;
- use conformed glass polygons instead of boxes.

Acceptance:

- the black silhouette reads as a compact rally hatch/sedan at 64 px;
- side, front, rear and rear-three-quarter views all read as the same design;
- no view resembles a pickup, SUV, kei truck or utility vehicle;
- the wheel diameter is visually dominant without becoming caricatured.

### 2. Model review harness

Capture at least:

- front three-quarter;
- pure side;
- rear three-quarter;
- chase/rear;
- full pack motion;
- suspension-compression and airborne frames.

Acceptance:

- no outline tears or doubled hull/Sobel lines;
- glass follows the body with no floating planes;
- tyres do not intersect the shell at full steer or suspension travel;
- the cockpit driver stays below the roof and inside the glass;
- hero and reduced-detail cars share the same silhouette.

### 3. Rally stage conversion

Audit every track section against a car-width envelope.

- widen high-speed and drift sections;
- preserve narrow exposure only where it is intentionally dangerous;
- replace bicycle-sized berm logic with entry/apex/exit space for slip angle;
- add ditches, road cuts, posts, tape, marker boards and gravel shoulders;
- reshape jumps into crests/compressions rather than bike lips;
- make the wrong-way and corner-preview systems use the revised road width.

Acceptance:

- two cars can run side by side in designated passing zones;
- the player can rotate a car without immediately leaving the ribbon;
- every obstacle has car-scale clearance;
- the road reads as a closed rally stage, not single-track trail.

### 4. Car footprint and handling language

- replace the bicycle-sized circular contact approximation with a car footprint;
- tune collision separation for a 1.86 m-wide, 4.10 m-long body;
- expose front/rear axle slip separately to FX and audio;
- increase readable countersteer and body settle without destabilising physics;
- make braking, throttle and handbrake produce distinct pitch/yaw signatures.

Acceptance:

- cars cannot overlap visibly in pack captures;
- a handbrake turn shows rotation, countersteer and recovery;
- braking produces front load and throttle produces rear squat;
- landing compression resolves axle, body and camera in a readable sequence.

### 5. Rally-specific camera and surface FX

- lower the default chase camera and bias it toward rear three-quarter;
- preserve the whole car at maximum yaw;
- generate dust at four contact patches;
- separate throttle spray, braking scrub, lateral gravel and landing bursts;
- scale stones and puffs to car tyres rather than bicycle tyres.

Acceptance:

- drift angle is visible without losing the road ahead;
- dust originates at wheels and responds to axle load/slip;
- the car never disappears behind its own plume;
- speed reads from road flow, suspension and surface reaction—not FOV alone.

### 6. Performance and cleanup

- merge static parts by material where it reduces draw calls;
- keep one geometry cache shared across all cars;
- use reduced detail for distant AI and ghost cars;
- retain deterministic capture output;
- delete superseded visual implementations after the new pass is approved.

Acceptance:

- production build and GLSL check pass;
- no per-frame allocations in model update;
- no new external runtime dependencies;
- no frame spike when four cars enter view, land or crash together.

## Review rule

A successful compile is only the floor. The PR remains draft until the visual reviewer cannot name a specific silhouette, stance, line-work or motion defect from the captured front/side/rear/chase set.
